# Who Knows?

**Who Knows?** is a first person mansion murder mystery. Its current subtitle is **Murder At Midnight Mansion**. Players explore the house, observe the other guests, compare evidence and testimony, and make an accusation. One or two guests may secretly be killers; the remaining guests try to identify them before the night ends. The game supports solo play, local two player split screen, and online lobbies with AI guests filling open seats.

The project's goal is a social deduction game in which each guest has a distinct way of noticing, interpreting, and discussing the same events. Character biography adds motive and voice, but it must never reveal a randomly assigned killer or override facts generated by the match. A player should be able to reason from what happened in the current round rather than from a fixed story answer.

## Current game

- Eight color coded guests take part in each match. Solo play has seven AI guests; local co-op has two players and six AI guests. Online lobbies currently admit up to six human players, with the other seats controlled by AI.
- The mansion has an art room, bathroom, conference room, game room, office, kitchen, dining room, library, lounge, a cellar, and connected tunnels. Players can move, inspect and carry objects, talk, search, keep notes, and accuse. The match engine controls roles, evidence, night phases, and outcomes.
- Five core weapons have fixed, room-appropriate homes. A kitchen knife, dungeon rope, and sword held by a hallway knight are secondary finds. One handed weapons can be carried together; a first strike wounds, leaves small blood drops, and can be treated with a bandage during the following night. A second strike kills. Two handed weapons kill in one strike.
- AI behavior uses authored traits from each guest's `soul.md`. The Websim chat runtime writes both the player's options and AI guests' replies in that guest's speech manner, using the current scene and recent conversation. The rules engine remains the source of factual claims, deliberate lies, and legal actions; factual rewrites can name only people and places in the supplied claim, and unrelated occupational details are rejected. The lie generator cannot invent a sighting of the person being addressed or a random location at the instant a night begins.
- Daily tasks (`js/clue/tasks.js`): each living savior rolls one of eleven chores each day (1 in 5 is No Task). Finishing it gives +1 trust with the other saviors; leaving it unfinished costs 1d4 trust with each of them. Killers get no chore. The dirt-stain chore spawns floor grime in a random room and nearby bleach and rag; a savior must bleach the stain and wipe it with the rag. Other saviors can use the same cleanup tools, so cleaning can look suspicious. The HUD shows a required kill on a deadline day, or an optional early kill and cleanup on other days, with the next required day.
- Physical tasks (`js/clue/world-task.js`, `js/clue/tasks3d.js`): humans interact with the station in the 3D room. They cut the kitchen lettuce, throw visible darts at the real board and retrieve missed sets, paint on the easel, scrub the bathroom vanity and mirror, sit at the office computer to click red defrag fragments, set the clock, sort books and papers, turn the gramophone crank, or aim at the real skull pool table. The host shares progress through `taskScene` and awards trust on completion; AI guests step through `workTask`.
- The butler (`js/clue/butler.js`, `butler3d.js`, `npc/butler/`): Mr. Grimsby appears only on days without an outstanding required kill, empties bins out the front door, mops assigned rooms with water and promptly mops hallway stains he sees, leaving blood traces that the Lens of Truth can reveal until a killer uses bleach; he never enters the cellar or tunnels and restocks bleach and towels in the bathroom. Asking him for information is a DC 18–20 roll; a failure gives a believable lie in the notes and a grudge he spreads to AI guests. Giving him the Lucky Coin earns one guaranteed truthful answer and an explanation of the coin. The coin respawns the day after an actual kill if none remains. AI guests seek him out via `seekButler` (after their task), weighted by soul traits (curiosity, skepticism, sociability, superstition for the coin trade, and `butlerDoubt` after catching a lie). They ask about their top suspect or the murder room and roll the same check as humans. Lies they believe go into their notes as `told:butler` facts.
- Dialogue context (`js/clue/observe.js`, `js/clue/talk-context.js`): a clear sighting records visible activity, held items, head direction, and a gaze target only if the witness can see both the person and target. Sampled running can record a visible heading, then the room entered if the same witness sees the transition. These facts, the current scene, and the speaker's match mood guide dialogue without turning a direction of travel into a claim that someone entered a room.
- Private thoughts (`js/clue/talk.js`, `js/clue/ui.js`): a played guest reacts in their own voice to touched cards, ashes, pocket searches, and periodically to new firsthand observations. Opening a task prepares separate AI thoughts for finishing and leaving. A witnessed note briefly glows and sends particles into the top-left Notes control. Killer thoughts know the player's role, objective, and confirmed personal attacks or body dragging; blood is treated as possible exposure of those acts rather than an unexplained clue. That context is sent only to the killer's own seat and is never a shareable notebook fact. Thoughts use the guest's speech manner and actual observed result; they appear only in that player's HUD. A shared cooldown spaces them out, and TTS never queues a backlog. Shared-screen play keeps thoughts silent so another player cannot hear private information.
- Perception (`Match.perceive` / `perceiveAt` in `js/clue/match.js`): sight is a forward view cone with clear sight within 60° and a fading edge to about 75°. It needs a clear line past walls and closed doors at every distance, including arm's length, and fades with distance (range 6 + 9 × observation metres, dimmer in overtime). Nothing behind a character is automatically seen. Faint glimpses drop activity and gaze detail; a run needs successive visible samples before it becomes a note. Third-party conversations are recorded only when both participants are in view; spoken claims that a player overhears retain their attributed source.
- Pushable chests (`PUSHABLES` / `placePushable` in `js/clue/layout.js`, `Match.pushFurniture`): the game room and library storage chests now sit clear of their doors. A "Push" target at the chest's end slides it along the wall in front of the door (door 8 for the game room, door 18 for the library), or back home. While a chest is more than halfway across, the door is held shut, `BLOCKED_DOORS` in `nav.js` removes it from pathfinding, and AI paths through it are dropped. Props resting on or stashed in the chest move with it. Snapshot field `push` holds each chest's slide value from 0 to 1.
- Honest sharing (`claimLine` in `js/clue/facts.js`): **Share** is reserved for facts the speaker personally witnessed or did. Facts told to the player or overheard become **Pass on** choices that name the source ("Mara told me that…"); deliberately fabricated claims are **Deceit**. A killer's regular dialogue offers at most one Share/Pass on choice and always two Deceit choices: a plausible false fact when possible plus a false promise about their intent, or two intent bluffs before enough time has passed for a plausible sighting lie. Known fabricated facts are not offered as truthful choices, and a character does not offer the listener a claim about that listener's own location. Seeing someone move a body records that action, not an unwitnessed murder.
- Patience (`Match.patience`): the timer begins only after the spoken line finishes and the reply options are visible, and resets with each exchange. Its baseline is 9 + 8×(1−restlessness) + 6×sociability + 3×(1−skepticism) + 3×trust seconds, adjusted for wounds, a known death that night, and the current conversational tone, then kept between 12 and 36 seconds. On expiry, a d20 plus trust/2 is rolled against 9 + restlessness×6 + skepticism×3 + 4 per grace already given. Success buys a shorter extra wait; failure ends the exchange and costs 0.5 trust. During a flicker, room-bound AI can approach and talk to people in the same room without leaving it.
- Flicker evacuation (`Match.routeToRefuge`): living guests outside a room follow the shortest reachable A* route into one, opening doors and climbing a hatch if needed. The doors seal after everyone arrives. An unreachable route expires instead of moving a character through walls; the late forced killer pursuit also walks a route to its victim.
- Killer peeks (`Match.peekRoom`): during a sealed killing flicker, a killer can peek at a closed door or up from a tunnel hatch. A d20 plus that guest's soul stealth modifier decides whether they privately note the occupants or are spotted and compelled to walk inside. A failed peek with occupants forces a kill; only guests with clear line of sight to both participants outside a blackout receive verified murder evidence and carry that witnessed accusation through later conversations and votes. Room membership alone does not confer certainty. AI killers peek at closed entrances and avoid crowded rooms after a successful look.
- Team-ups (`js/clue/teamup.js`): after at least one exchange, a random chance can add a Team up choice. The choice is a persuasion roll. If it fails, the other guest's own soul declines with its `teamNo` line. If it succeeds against a human, that player still chooses Accept or Decline, and declining has no penalty. AI partners follow you. A pair that stays in sight all day gains trust and a `team` alibi fact, which affects AI suspicion and accusation rebuttals. A team-up lasts one day.
- Fire (`js/clue/burning.js`, `js/clue/burn3d.js`): in a lit fireplace or on a lit stove, rags, towels, bandages, rope, the flashlight (which melts), and paper, books, pillows, logs, boxes, bread, candles and umbrellas smoulder for 5 s. During that time they can be picked back up. They then burn for 15 s and leave ash. Guests in the room smell the burning, and anyone who can see the fire notes what is in it. A bloodied cloth leaves a smell in the room for the rest of the day. Sifting ash is a d20 roll based on observation and curiosity. Your own guest then describes the result. Witnesses see thrown objects and where they land.
- Each guest has a shipped `Memory.json` with starting relationships and background memories. New match memories are saved in the visitor's browser under `who-knows.memory.<color>`. This rebrand begins a fresh set of local character memories; older `clue.memory.*` browser data is left untouched and is not loaded into the new cast.
- The title screen may show a live match or an AI demonstration. Online play uses the project's realtime room and a host authoritative match.
- A title-screen Help control opens a page-based casebook covering roles, evidence, objects, rooms, controls, play modes, and each guest's complete authored soul and starting memories. The casebook renders snapshots from the actual game meshes for items, overhead room views, guest models, and ghosts; it also shows the in-game stamina bar. Character lore comes from the existing `npc/` files so writing changes appear without duplicated copy.

### Speech authority and dialogue meaning

Conversation speech, readiness, chosen actions and closure require active participant membership. NPC speech reports also require a host-issued, single-use token for the current reply. Nearby listeners acknowledging an NPC-to-NPC pocket exchange use their explicit acknowledgement set and stage token; they are intentionally not conversation participants.

Evidence-bearing NPC lines, firsthand/hearsay choices and fabricated factual claims retain their canonical rules-engine wording. Factual content is not freely paraphrased: source, person, victim, negation, uncertainty and any legitimately known clock time remain intact. Social lines may be generated, with conservative checks for meaning, topic, certainty, question shape, added observations and length. Rejected wording falls back whole rather than losing a clause to truncation. NPC evidence avoids an unnecessary wording request, but is still spoken with TTS.

Killer intent bluffs use ordinary practical cooperation (staying nearby, checking rooms, comparing notes). Prompts prohibit conspicuous assurances, admissions, exaggerated innocence and occupational flourishes. Deceit labels describe the choosing player's private action; the label is not spoken to the listener. The engine still resolves deception using character stats and known evidence; plausible wording does not guarantee a successful lie.

### Remote movement

Clients retain immediate input prediction and the normal send cadence. Host validation checks finite values, ordered sequence numbers, bounds, collisions and a replenishing movement-distance budget capped at current maximum run/ghost speeds (halved while dragging). Client-supplied teleport flags grant no bypass. The bounded burst allowance tolerates ordinary packet jitter without adding an input buffer.

Only rejected or materially conflicting moves produce sequenced corrections. The receiver preserves input since that sample instead of rewinding to an old raw position. Teleports, bumps and reconnection reset relevant history. Impossible movement is corrected, so this is not a promise of zero correction under every latency or collision condition. The budget enforces maximum movement speed; it is not a server-side replacement for the entire stamina system. Hands and throw vectors are also checked for finite, bounded input.

### Loose props and gravity

Loose props use gravity, substeps, support checks, friction and sleep. Sleeping bodies wake after support loss. Prop contacts now use local furniture parts that follow live furniture position/rotation: seats/backrests/legs, actual stair steps, table tops/legs, and narrower lamp/stand parts. Stool support no longer includes an invisible backrest. These replace broad whole-object walker boxes for those furniture classes. Sleeping requires support after final collision resolution; gone props do not simulate.

Public prop deltas are retained until network transmission so peers receive final resting positions. Visible prop meshes continue to seat their bases against their physics contact plane. This is still approximate sphere/part physics, not exact concave mesh collision for every decorative object. Gameplay/visual retesting will identify any remaining asset-specific offsets.

## Database diagnostics and agent review

Diagnostics run automatically in local and online play, including startup. They use the project's server/database, not comments or browser-only storage. Runtime events and performance reports share a random page session ID and build ID; round IDs and conversation traces allow related events to be followed. No dialogue text, pocket contents, world coordinates, account identity or credentials are accepted by the structured diagnostic schema.

### What is recorded

- Startup stage durations, readiness and failure class.
- Frame intervals (p50/p95/p99, maximum, count over 50 ms), CPU work, update/render stage timings, resource/draw counts and long tasks. Worst entries include long frame intervals even when CPU work was short. These are not GPU timer measurements.
- Dialogue generation duration, invalid JSON, timeout/failure and fallback reasons; canonical evidence preservation; visible foreground wait duration and conversation stage.
- Speech preparation duration, playback success/unavailability/cancellation, engine and queue depth.
- Rejected conversation actions, movement rejection/correction/reconciliation and lost prop support.
- Page visibility changes and runtime error class/source location without message contents.

Events buffer up to 128 rows and send up to 24 per batch about every 10 seconds. Final flush is attempted on hide/page exit. Stable event IDs make retries idempotent; failed batches stay queued while the page is alive. Overflow is counted. Delivery after browser termination is not guaranteed. The server sanitizes again, bounds request size, and limits each session to 360 batches/hour. Retention is 14 days / 50,000 diagnostic rows, pruned periodically on writes.

Performance windows are sent every 30 visible seconds, with a high 2,000-successful-report page cap instead of stopping after 40 attempted requests. Their separate table retains 7 days / 10,000 reports. A failed write does not consume the client success quota. Existing online connection/session logs remain separate.

## Voting, spoken hair permission and controller update

### Eligible votes and disproved combinations

Every proposer and voter must choose a **living guest other than themselves**. Murdered guests and caught killers (including the first caught killer in a two-killer match) cannot be selected again. The host enforces this on human requests, NPC choices, timeout ballots and tally; hiding a button alone is insufficient.

A wrong revealed **person + weapon + room** combination becomes collective disproved knowledge. The exact triple cannot be submitted again while that knowledge remains; individual people, weapons and rooms are not banned. NPCs choose an eligible alternative. The UI explains why a repeated triple is disabled. Only discovery of a previously undiscovered body clears this set, not a new day, an undiscovered kill, or repeating an old body's report. Public snapshots retain this non-secret knowledge for recovery.

The table still tallies categories separately. A majority can therefore assemble an already disproved triple from individually valid ballots. The first such outcome reopens the whole vote. If the same problem repeats, the table uses the most supported eligible complete ballot already cast and announces that fallback, preventing an endless known-wrong re-vote. If a locked person category names a voter themselves, that guest abstains on the person field while retaining their weapon/room choices; they never cast a self-vote.

### Required kill slots and optional early kills

Killers may complete a murder on any exploration day, with the existing limit of **one completed murder per day shared by both killers**. Ordinary reach, target, room, weapon and wound constraints remain. Required deadlines are anchored at **2, 4, 6…** for one killer and **3, 6, 9…** for two killers (including two-killer local co-op). The interval follows killer count, not simply whether the game is online.

A completed kill satisfies the upcoming deadline and advances to the next anchored slot. For example, a solo kill on day 1 or 2 makes the next required day 4; a further early kill on day 3 makes it day 6. With two killers, a kill on day 1, 2 or 3 makes the next deadline day 6. A wound alone does not satisfy a slot; an actual fatal bleed-out does. Deaths cannot leave a required deadline in the past. At the end of an optional day there is no forced kill; on an unmet deadline the existing mandatory flicker/pursuit applies. HUD task text and role/help information show the changed rule.

### Hair permission is spoken dialogue

The haircut invitation pop-up is removed. Asking from an interaction or a Share task choice starts a conversation request. The request finishes TTS/captions before the share-task roll and the recipient's explicit **Allow / Refuse** choices. Human recipients make that choice themselves; NPC recipients consider the roll, personality and trust, and can decline. Permission is never inferred merely because a roll succeeded.

Each character has `hairAsk`, `hairYes` and `hairNo` soul lines. The response is spoken before either character starts walking to the bathroom chair. Refusal ends the exchange without routing anyone. Tokens, participant acknowledgements and bounded deadlines prevent stale speech from advancing it. Split-screen participants share one audible line with separate caption observers. Manual cutting, cancellation and the existing killer betrayal continue after consent; sneak cutting remains its separate stealth action.

## Core Cards, evidence and time knowledge (fork v4)

Three private Core Card slots occupy the Today’s Story hand at the top left. Their portrait-shaped faces show category icons; detail is revealed on hover, keyboard focus, or tap. Notes → Core Cards supports a fourth slot, manual recompilation (10-second cooldown), supporting-note inspection, and restoring still-valid archived cards into chosen slots. Core Cards replace the original first-night story checklist; there is no separate touch-to-collect counter or NPC weapon-collection routine. Unfilled slots remain dormant card backs; no question or routine sighting is promoted merely to fill the hand. Cards remain round-local character beliefs: an **Alibi account is not proof of innocence**, and a belief score is a game heuristic, not a calibrated probability of guilt.

The host compiles observations, evidence, alibis, suspects and accusations from that character’s own notebook. Direct observation, visual clarity, source trust, successful persuasion, skepticism, observation, memory and contradictions affect weighting. A weak pocket glimpse stays uncertain. One fact key contributes once even if a rumor circulates repeatedly. Received deception is assessed as an account the character heard, without consulting the engine’s hidden lie flag. The engine retains structured source attribution; private cards and support are sent only to their owning seat.

Accusation cards require **strictly greater than 75%** support and a complete person / room / weapon tuple. A genuine clear line-of-sight murder observation alone earns **Witness · 100%**; room membership, a blackout, a body discovery, hearsay and a victim’s ghost knowledge do not confer Witness status. Inferred accusations combine person, weapon and room support. A known assault on this victim is strong evidence; a body, a matching bloodied weapon and an observed armed suspect can also persuade a less skeptical soul. Discovery location is a fallible room hypothesis: an undetected relocation or planted object can mislead a savior. The compiler never checks the engine’s real murder location to correct a character. Dead suspects and already-disproved combinations cannot remain active accusations. Historical notes remain available.

During day one, generation is locked until 60 seconds of active daytime simulation (excluding intro/loading/paused simulation); Notes continue collecting throughout. After that, the host checks one guest every 1.5 seconds in a round-robin. A guest with changed notes can recompile at most once per 12 seconds, skipping their active conversation. Bounded AI review can be queued at most once per 45 seconds per guest; deterministic updates remain available without AI. Manual slot selections survive these periodic opening-day updates, while an explicit recompile or the next daily compilation releases them. Unchanged card faces are not rebuilt, preserving hover/focus detail. Daily compilation begins when tasks settle at day end. Work is spread between guests. Deterministic ranking supplies usable cards immediately for each guest; a serial background AI queue can rank existing candidate IDs with a six-second selection deadline per job; if the underlying SDK request remains outstanding, the queue waits for it to settle to avoid accumulating requests. The model cannot author a clue, invent an ID, change a score or award Witness status. AI failure leaves the deterministic selection intact. Manual recompilation/equipping changes a revision so an older queued or in-flight rank cannot overwrite the player’s choice. Leaving the match stops queued card work. Background jobs do not slow the shared countdown.

Dialogue evidence selection and choice ranking favor active cards while direct questions and relevant answers keep priority. Both speakers receive their own card context, never their partner’s private cards. Structured factual clauses remain canonical to protect attribution, negation, identities and uncertainty; character-specific account templates and the existing soul voice guide provide variation. This is a hybrid game-memory system, not unrestricted generative storytelling or an attempt to replicate BG3’s authored dialogue production.

### Task investigation

Completing the assigned daily task triggers one automatic room-investigation check. Remaining day time, measured duration, measured progress regressions and observation/curiosity stats affect the d20 check. Dart quality is calculated from bounded dart positions rather than accepting the reported score for investigation quality. Where trustworthy attempt telemetry is absent, quality and efficiency are neutral, not perfect. This is a quality proxy; it does not fully grade the artistic merits of a painting or every mini-game interaction.

A success may reveal a real point of interest, such as a storage container holding a concealed body, and invokes normal perception for visible evidence. The clue does not disclose hidden contents, the murderer or a weapon: the container must still be opened/searched. A failed roll produces a soul-informed private reflection without a discovery. Optional AI chooses among safe private reflections; it cannot invent the search result. Investigation metadata is diagnostic, but clue and dialogue text are never uploaded to telemetry.

### Character time knowledge

Simulation timestamps remain available to engine scheduling. Spoken/notebook accounts no longer automatically expose them. Ivo’s authored pocket watch is represented by `has_watch: 1` in his soul. Other guests need an accurate clock they can actually see; the dining clock is not reliable until the clock task is completed. Memories preserve the time source they actually had, including a source-attributed time learned from someone else. Without a reading, accounts omit exact times, time-based contradictions are not inferred, and questions ask about events rather than manufacturing an exact time of death. Clock knowledge is independent of the player’s countdown UI.

### Research and implementation boundaries

- [Generative Agents (Park et al., 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442): retrieval, reflection and planning motivate separating stored observations from higher-level priorities. Their reported memory/retrieval and embellishment failures motivate source-linked cards and deterministic factual constraints here.
- [LIGHT (Urbanek et al., 2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03094): grounded persona, environment and action context motivate providing the current conversation and only the character’s available knowledge.

New modules: `core-cards.js`, `core-card-ui.js`, `time-knowledge.js`, `task-investigation.js`, `room-beliefs.js`, `trace-survey.js`. Diagnostic build: `clue-core-memories-20261019-c`; events `core.compiled`, `core.ranking`, `core.trace_search`, `task.investigation`, with compile/ranking duration and bounded search counters. Existing owner diagnostic endpoints can filter these events without storing private notebook contents. Reconnecting online seats receive only their own journal/cards; journal replay arrives in a single private batch, renders once and suppresses duplicate inner thoughts. Archives are paginated in Notes, and background ranking yields while an active conversation is underway. Offline continuation retains the local seat’s already-known notes/cards without recreating other guests’ private notebooks. Gameplay verification is pending player testing; source syntax checks do not establish gameplay correctness or performance.

### Fallible room beliefs and staged scenes

A **Location theory** card distinguishes “the body was found here” from “the killing may have happened here.” Room support starts from the character’s source-weighted discovery account. It can be wrong. Soul skepticism, observation and memory change how the character weighs corroboration and uncertainty. A clear murder witness keeps the witnessed room at 100%; other room theories stay below certainty. NPC voting uses the same room reasoning, and players/NPCs can speak an explicitly uncertain core theory. A received theory remains an opinion and cannot bootstrap itself into physical proof.

Only personally observed traces or received, attributed notes affect reasoning. Visible blood pools, droplets and smears are distinguished; water-cleaned traces require an actually held Lens. Dirt from a grime task is excluded from blood perception. Observed dragging weakens confidence in the discovery room; a room-to-room transfer is recorded only after the observer sees the body continuously across that transition. Transfer does not magically identify where the attack began. Bloodied cleaning items and bleach suggest possible cleanup weakly, with capped effects; they do not identify whose blood it was, and innocent chores remain possible. No invisible victim identity or true murder room is used to disambiguate a trace.

A lack of notes is **not** evidence of an absence. After a character knows about a body in the current room, actual clear floor observations accumulate an approximate, unobstructed grid coverage per day and search method. A negative search note requires several seconds and records its limited coverage, whether blood was seen and whether the Lens was in hand. Only adequate coverage can reduce support based on missing blood; a Lens-assisted search weighs more when its function is known. The strongest search replaces weaker overlapping searches in the inference rather than stacking their negative results. A past blood observation is not erased by a later clean floor. Missing blood has less meaning without a plausible bleeding mechanism; bleach, missed areas and removal remain alternatives. Owning or knowing about the Lens does not create search coverage.

A body observer learns only a weapon actually visible near that body, if any. The engine’s hidden killing weapon is no longer copied automatically into a finder’s body note after relocation. A nearby weapon can be planted and is not labelled a confirmed murder weapon. Other guests can form their own direct observations of an already-discovered body when they actually see it.

These are tunable game heuristics, not a forensic probability model. [The Forensic Science Regulator’s incident-scene guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/incident-scene-examination-fsr-gui-0006/incident-scene-examination-accessible) discusses context-dependent examination, recording relevant presence/absence, and judgments based on information available at the time. That informs the distinction between observed findings, search limitations and hypotheses here; numerical weights are authored gameplay values. Staging scenarios and character differences still need player evaluation.

### Today’s Story card presentation (fork v5)

Core Cards now replace both the old text block and the original Today’s Story collection system. The default hand is three portrait cards, with a fourth available in Notes. Card faces contain vector icon combinations: eyes for observations, shield/person for alibis, person/question for suspects, magnifier/clue for investigation, person/room/weapon for accusations, and eye/star for a verified witness. Daily and manual compilation continue to use the same source-weighted evidence rules. Dialogue topics now come from notebook observations rather than an old collected-card inventory. ### Useful opening memories (fork v6)

Core Cards are no longer individual high-confidence routine sightings. Reliability and importance are separate: merely being sure someone stood in a hallway does not make that a major clue. A new `core-memories.js` compiler merges the character’s own sustained room observations (at least 20 simulation seconds of recorded coverage) into a Room visit memory. Room durations use qualitative language without inventing clock readings. Repeated records of one visit do not create separate cards.

Clear overlapping observations of another guest for at least 12 recorded seconds can produce a Company memory. A recorded conversation with that person at the location supports an explicitly **potential** Alibi: they may corroborate part of the account, but the compiler does not assume they noticed us, remember us, or can clear either character for the whole night. It never reads that other character’s private notebook to manufacture corroboration. Brief self sightings, routine standing/lingering, ordinary chores and innocent handling do not occupy core slots. Significant real traces, a missing weapon, a wound, a body or a verified witness remain eligible once generation unlocks.

The hand may contain fewer than three actual cards, even after the first minute. Empty slots show inactive backs, not fabricated questions or forced low-value observations. Automatic selection avoids redundant support and limits repeated categories; the archive and manual selections remain available. A guest in conversation waits until free for an automatic recompile. The model may rank fewer than three supported candidate IDs and still cannot invent a card.

Room-specific vector symbols replace identical eye faces: a library book, kitchen pan, dining plate, art brush, office desk, conference table, game piece, bathroom tub and lounge sofa distinguish locations. An hourglass represents a room stay rather than a known clock time. Company and alibi cards add person/shield symbols and a colored companion marker. A compact category label reduces ambiguity; detailed prose remains on hover/focus/tap. Notes includes a symbol legend. Support percentages on these cards refer to recollection, not innocence or guilt.

Research: [Generative Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442) supports separating raw observations from synthesized memories; [NN/G’s icon-usability guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/icon-usability/) informs the recognizable symbols and short category labels. The thresholds and ranking are authored game heuristics, not values validated by those publications. Gameplay and visual checks remain pending player evaluation.


### Awareness, flicker reasoning and private strategy (fork v7)

Ordinary conversation remains social until a public body discovery, personally known attack or a genuinely missed expected contact supplies concern. A planned companion unseen for 45 active seconds can be recorded as missing; simply being elsewhere does not expose hidden deaths. Seeing the companion again resolves that concern. Opening memory dialogue uses a short personal recollection, rather than reading a Core Card's legalistic qualification about innocence. Peaceful exchanges omit the forensic Core prompt and unsolicited pocket demands.

Sealed flicker records each survivor's actually visible company and weapons. Occlusion, darkness, incapacitation, room changes and interrupted watches retain uncertainty. A weapon must remain visible, unmoved and unhandled across the recorded watch to support a continuous-watch claim. A once-per-second visible-floor grid records approximate spatial scan coverage separately from watch duration. A visible patch is not a full room search, and even a full floor scan does not certify concealed areas or continuous safety. These memories receive high salience, not automatic certainty, and trigger recompilation when flicker ends or discovery interrupts it. A post-kill flicker watch never clears an earlier killing. The metadata's phase timing is not disclosed as knowledge of an undiscovered murder. Coverage percentages describe the recorded watch, not guilt probabilities. The first-minute opening eligibility rule remains.

Killer Core Cards distinguish private objectives (cover, plausible framing, known witness threat) from evidence. Invented framing stays a lie, never a factual note. NPC killers prioritize a known witness at the next legal opportunity without bypassing the one-kill-per-day rule; a witness is known only if the killer perceived them or received their firsthand account. Actual firsthand murder witnesses receive +3 to persuasion checks; deception and physical pocket contests do not get that bonus.

Voting guidance is private per seat. Self/dead choices and genuinely conclusive exclusions are disabled on the host as well as struck out in the UI. A verified sole-killer witness rules out other people; weapon/room components are hard-excluded only for a unique first-day witnessed case, because later unresolved murders may use different combinations. Disproved full tuples stay excluded until a new body discovery resets them. Recommendations are beliefs; killers instead receive a plausible framing target protecting their partner.

### Persistent game audit archive

`game_audit` is a separate append-only database table, not gameplay authority. It records build, shared round ID, page session, stable deduplication event ID, UTC reporting time and bounded structured detail. Events include canonical and spoken dialogue, generated choices, Core Card summaries/support keys, flicker coverage, persuasion rolls, kills, votes and outcomes. Existing performance diagnostics remain separate. Host truth is labelled and stays in owner-readable diagnostics, never shared player snapshots. No microphone recordings or account credentials are collected.

Reporting is asynchronous, batched every five seconds, size-bounded and retried with stable IDs. IndexedDB retains pending reports per project across reloads where available; queued sessions are submitted separately. The in-memory pending limit is 1,000 events; overflow is marked as a gap. Closing the browser, blocked storage, connectivity failures or clients omitting/tampering with reports can cause gaps. Reports are explicitly `client_report` and `partial`, not guaranteed authoritative evidence. Title-demo host events are excluded. No historical sessions can be reconstructed retroactively.

Owner-only review routes use Websim's injected owner/user headers:

- `GET /api/debug/game-rounds`: latest 200 round summaries; use `before=<nextBefore>` for older pages.
- `GET /api/debug/game-audit?round=<uuid>&after=<id>&limit=500`: ascending event pages, with optional build/kind/session filters. Start with `after=0`; continue with `nextAfter`. Without `after`, returns the newest records.
- `POST /api/game-audit`: validated, bounded append ingestion with stable-ID deduplication and per-session throttling.

Audit records currently have no automatic expiry. Storage grows with play and needs periodic owner review. This does not schedule automatic agent monitoring. Static module parsing was performed; gameplay, multi-client privacy behavior, deployment database migration and visual behavior still require player evaluation.

### Flashlight travel during flicker (fork v10)

A living, conscious guest holding the existing Flashlight in either hand can traverse the hallway and basement/tunnels during flicker. The host checks actual hand membership and prop ownership, not client claims. Pocketed, dropped, gone or merely nearby lights do not count; torches retain their illumination behavior but do not grant this permission. Existing killer movement privileges remain.

A current, reciprocal, accepted team-up shares protection while the companion is within five metres, on the same floor and has a clear path to the flashlight carrier. This does not require looking directly at one another. An unteamed guest gains no privilege. A tightly scoped three-second hand-off at the same ladder lets the partner follow across floors while the carrier stays near the other endpoint. Losing the light, incapacitation, separation, a blocked path or a broken team removes protection. NPC partners use the regular pathfinder, door handling and ladders to follow; they do not start chores during this night escort.

On protection loss, Saviors immediately switch to host-driven fearful retreat. They choose the nearest reachable refuge by actual path length, climb out if needed, and close that room's doors on arrival. Already sheltered guests close their doors and stay. Regaining protection cancels the fear route. Protected explorers no longer prevent the flicker from sealing, and client prediction/door/hatch prompts use the host's public travel-permission list. Door obstructions, body collision and no-murder-in-tunnels rules remain. Permission is not invulnerability.

A fixed spotlight follows the held Flashlight, with bounded 512px shadows on shadow-enabled graphics settings. Its shader light count stays constant; no light objects are allocated per frame. Host-side directional illumination contributes to darkness perception and tunnel visibility through LOS checks. Floor-changing watches remain uncertain rather than certifying the starting room. Protection transitions are included in owner audit events as `flicker.travel`. Help covers the feature in both its short lighting instructions and detailed rules.

Edited modules passed static module syntax parsing. Multiplayer, ladder hand-offs, fearful retreat routes and visual/performance behavior still require playtesting.
