# Historical development notes through Clue v45

These notes describe earlier implementations and their recorded checks. They are not claims about validation of this fork. See the root README for current behavior.

# Who Knows?

**Who Knows?** is a first person mansion murder mystery. Its current subtitle is **Murder At Midnight Mansion**. Players explore the house, observe the other guests, compare evidence and testimony, and make an accusation. One or two guests may secretly be killers; the remaining guests try to identify them before the night ends. The game supports solo play, local two player split screen, and online lobbies with AI guests filling open seats.

The project's goal is a social deduction game in which each guest has a distinct way of noticing, interpreting, and discussing the same events. Character biography adds motive and voice, but it must never reveal a randomly assigned killer or override facts generated by the match. A player should be able to reason from what happened in the current round rather than from a fixed story answer.

## Current game

- Eight color coded guests take part in each match. Solo play has seven AI guests; local co-op has two players and six AI guests. Online lobbies currently admit up to six human players, with the other seats controlled by AI.
- The mansion has an art room, bathroom, conference room, game room, office, kitchen, dining room, library, lounge, a cellar, and connected tunnels. Players can move, inspect and carry objects, talk, search, keep notes, and accuse. The match engine controls roles, evidence, night phases, and outcomes.
- Five core weapons have fixed, room-appropriate homes. A kitchen knife, dungeon rope, and sword held by a hallway knight are secondary finds. One handed weapons can be carried together; a first strike wounds, leaves small blood drops, and can be treated with a bandage during the following night. A second strike kills. Two handed weapons kill in one strike.
- AI behavior uses authored traits from each guest's `soul.md`. The Websim chat runtime writes both the player's options and AI guests' replies in that guest's speech manner, using the current scene and recent conversation. The rules engine remains the source of factual claims, deliberate lies, and legal actions; factual rewrites can name only people and places in the supplied claim, and unrelated occupational details are rejected. The lie generator cannot invent a sighting of the person being addressed or a random location at the instant a night begins.
- Daily tasks (`js/clue/tasks.js`): each living savior rolls one of eleven chores each day (1 in 5 is No Task). Finishing it gives +1 trust with the other saviors; leaving it unfinished costs 1d4 trust with each of them. Killers get no chore. The dirt-stain chore spawns floor grime in a random room and nearby bleach and rag; a savior must bleach the stain and wipe it with the rag. Other saviors can use the same cleanup tools, so cleaning can look suspicious. On a kill day the HUD shows a required kill; on other days it shows optional blood/body cleanup.
- Physical tasks (`js/clue/world-task.js`, `js/clue/tasks3d.js`): humans interact with the station in the 3D room. They cut the kitchen lettuce, throw visible darts at the real board and retrieve missed sets, paint on the easel, scrub the bathroom vanity and mirror, sit at the office computer to click red defrag fragments, set the clock, sort books and papers, turn the gramophone crank, or aim at the real skull pool table. The host shares progress through `taskScene` and awards trust on completion; AI guests step through `workTask`.
- The butler (`js/clue/butler.js`, `butler3d.js`, `npc/butler/`): Mr. Grimsby appears only on non-kill days, empties bins out the front door, mops assigned rooms with water and promptly mops hallway stains he sees, leaving blood traces that the Lens of Truth can reveal until a killer uses bleach; he never enters the cellar or tunnels and restocks bleach and towels in the bathroom. Asking him for information is a DC 18–20 roll; a failure gives a believable lie in the notes and a grudge he spreads to AI guests. Giving him the Lucky Coin earns one guaranteed truthful answer and an explanation of the coin. The coin respawns only the day after a kill day if none remains. AI guests seek him out via `seekButler` (after their task), weighted by soul traits (curiosity, skepticism, sociability, superstition for the coin trade, and `butlerDoubt` after catching a lie). They ask about their top suspect or the murder room and roll the same check as humans. Lies they believe go into their notes as `told:butler` facts.
- Dialogue context (`js/clue/observe.js`, `js/clue/talk-context.js`): a clear sighting records visible activity, held items, head direction, and a gaze target only if the witness can see both the person and target. Sampled running can record a visible heading, then the room entered if the same witness sees the transition. These facts, the current scene, and the speaker's match mood guide dialogue without turning a direction of travel into a claim that someone entered a room.
- Private thoughts (`js/clue/talk.js`, `js/clue/ui.js`): a played guest reacts in their own voice to touched cards, ashes, pocket searches, and periodically to new firsthand observations. Opening a task prepares separate AI thoughts for finishing and leaving. A witnessed note briefly glows and sends particles into the top-left Notes control. Killer thoughts know the player's role, objective, and confirmed personal attacks or body dragging; blood is treated as possible exposure of those acts rather than an unexplained clue. That context is sent only to the killer's own seat and is never a shareable notebook fact. Thoughts use the guest's speech manner and actual observed result; they appear only in that player's HUD. A shared cooldown spaces them out, and TTS never queues a backlog. Shared-screen play keeps thoughts silent so another player cannot hear private information.
- Perception (`Match.perceive` / `perceiveAt` in `js/clue/match.js`): sight is a forward view cone with clear sight within 60° and a fading edge to about 75°. It needs a clear line past walls and closed doors at every distance, including arm's length, and fades with distance (range 6 + 9 × observation metres, dimmer in overtime). Nothing behind a character is automatically seen. Faint glimpses drop activity and gaze detail; a run needs successive visible samples before it becomes a note. Third-party conversations are recorded only when both participants are in view; spoken claims that a player overhears retain their attributed source.
- Pushable chests (`PUSHABLES` / `placePushable` in `js/clue/layout.js`, `Match.pushFurniture`): the game room and library storage chests now sit clear of their doors. A "Push" target at the chest's end slides it along the wall in front of the door (door 8 for the game room, door 18 for the library), or back home. While a chest is more than halfway across, the door is held shut, `BLOCKED_DOORS` in `nav.js` removes it from pathfinding, and AI paths through it are dropped. Props resting on or stashed in the chest move with it. Snapshot field `push` holds each chest's slide value from 0 to 1.
- Honest sharing (`claimLine` in `js/clue/facts.js`): **Share** is reserved for facts the speaker personally witnessed or did. Facts told to the player or overheard become **Pass on** choices that name the source ("Mara told me that…"); deliberately fabricated claims are **Deceit**. A killer's regular dialogue offers at most one Share/Pass on choice and always two Deceit choices: a plausible false fact when possible plus a false promise about their intent, or two intent bluffs before enough time has passed for a plausible sighting lie. Known fabricated facts are not offered as truthful choices, and a character does not offer the listener a claim about that listener's own location. Seeing someone move a body records that action, not an unwitnessed murder.
- Patience (`Match.patience`): the timer begins only after the spoken line finishes and the reply options are visible, and resets with each exchange. Its baseline is 9 + 8×(1−restlessness) + 6×sociability + 3×(1−skepticism) + 3×trust seconds, adjusted for wounds, a known death that night, and the current conversational tone, then kept between 12 and 36 seconds. On expiry, a d20 plus trust/2 is rolled against 9 + restlessness×6 + skepticism×3 + 4 per grace already given. Success buys a shorter extra wait; failure ends the exchange and costs 0.5 trust. During a flicker, room-bound AI can approach and talk to people in the same room without leaving it.
- Flicker evacuation (`Match.routeToRefuge`): living guests outside a room follow the shortest reachable A* route into one, opening doors and climbing a hatch if needed. The doors seal after everyone arrives. An unreachable route expires instead of moving a character through walls; the late forced killer pursuit also walks a route to its victim.
- Killer peeks (`Match.peekRoom`): during a sealed killing flicker, a killer can peek at a closed door or up from a tunnel hatch. A d20 plus that guest's soul stealth modifier decides whether they privately note the occupants or are spotted and compelled to walk inside. A failed peek with occupants forces a kill; surviving witnesses receive direct murder evidence, retain permanent certainty about the killer, and carry that exact accusation through later conversations and votes. AI killers peek at closed entrances and avoid crowded rooms after a successful look.
- Team-ups (`js/clue/teamup.js`): after at least one exchange, a random chance can add a Team up choice. The choice is a persuasion roll. If it fails, the other guest's own soul declines with its `teamNo` line. If it succeeds against a human, that player still chooses Accept or Decline, and declining has no penalty. AI partners follow you. A pair that stays in sight all day gains trust and a `team` alibi fact, which affects AI suspicion and accusation rebuttals. A team-up lasts one day.
- Fire (`js/clue/burning.js`, `js/clue/burn3d.js`): in a lit fireplace or on a lit stove, rags, towels, bandages, rope, the flashlight (which melts), and paper, books, pillows, logs, boxes, bread, candles and umbrellas smoulder for 5 s. During that time they can be picked back up. They then burn for 15 s and leave ash. Guests in the room smell the burning, and anyone who can see the fire notes what is in it. A bloodied cloth leaves a smell in the room for the rest of the day. Sifting ash is a d20 roll based on observation and curiosity. Your own guest then describes the result. Witnesses see thrown objects and where they land.
- Each guest has a shipped `Memory.json` with starting relationships and background memories. New match memories are saved in the visitor's browser under `who-knows.memory.<color>`. This rebrand begins a fresh set of local character memories; older `clue.memory.*` browser data is left untouched and is not loaded into the new cast.
- The title screen may show a live match or an AI demonstration. Online play uses the project's realtime room and a host authoritative match.
- A title-screen Help control opens a page-based casebook covering roles, evidence, objects, rooms, controls, play modes, and each guest's complete authored soul and starting memories. The casebook renders snapshots from the actual game meshes for items, overhead room views, guest models, and ghosts; it also shows the in-game stamina bar. Character lore comes from the existing `npc/` files so writing changes appear without duplicated copy.

## Project structure

- `index.html` is the served entry point. It holds the screen markup and the current branding.
- `css/clue.css` styles the screens, logo, HUD, and menus.
- `js/clue/main.js` starts the game, local character selection, demo, and online lobby screens.
- `js/clue/data.js` contains the cast, room grid, items, and game constants.
- `js/clue/match.js` is the authoritative match simulation. Other `js/clue/` modules implement rendering, movement, input, UI, AI behavior, and dialogue.
- `js/clue/souls.js` loads each guest's soul and memory; `js/clue/talk.js` frames AI dialogue while preserving known facts.
- `js/clue/manual.js` supplies the casebook pages and reads character lore; `js/clue/manual-visuals.js` renders lazy image previews from game meshes; `css/manual.css` styles the casebook and animated title-screen Help control.
- `server.js` relays realtime online lobby and match messages. It retains a six human player lobby limit.
- Online session events are stored in the private `session_logs` database table for signed-in players. The project owner can inspect recent events at `/api/debug/logs` and filter by `?code=ABCD`, `?session=<id>`, `?user=<user-id>`, or `?event=start-seen` (up to `?limit=200`). Join attempts, accepted starts, delivery failures, client receipt, initialization, and timeouts share lobby and session IDs.
- `models/`, `audio/`, `sfx/`, and `uploads/` contain the assets used by the current project. See `uploads/ASSET-CREDITS.md` and `uploads/ASSET-CREDITS-2.md` for existing asset notes.
- `legacy/` is an earlier, separate prototype. It is not the active game and was not part of this rebrand.

There is no package installation or build step. The project is served directly from this directory, and `index.html` loads the JavaScript modules in the browser. The game uses Three.js from the import map in `index.html` and Websim runtime services for online play and optional AI dialogue.

## Hair and barber chair

All human grooms contain cuttable fibers with stable IDs, metric lengths,
movement-relative wind, flexible guide physics and body/arm collision. Robot
forms never instantiate hair. Localized cuts persist in match snapshots and
shorten the active guides. See `models/HAIRCUTS.md` for protocol and limitations.

Cut Hair requires the Kitchen Knife. Share the task to roll for permission;
a human target must explicitly Accept. Both guests route to the bathroom barber
chair, where manual strokes must remove 15% of the original groom's total length.
Sneak cutting is a stealth d20 opposed by the target's observation/attention,
with a penalty if seen. Dead humans and robots are ineligible. Assignments need
two living human-form guests with hair remaining and are waived without penalty
if no valid target survives. A consenting chair session gives a killer a private
betrayal option, governed by the existing kill-day and one-kill rules. The host
validates consent/session, actual knife, forms, reach, line of sight and cuts.

## Spoken faces

Each human head has 15 retargeted CC0 viseme morphs. `audio.js` plays returned TTS
audio URLs and uses the actual audio clock and envelope; `lip-motion.js` blends
mouth shapes with fast attacks, smooth releases and coarticulation. Silence and
private narration close the mouth. Spoken player choices and AI replies share
a queue so a reply does not interrupt the player's line. Timing within the line
is inferred from text because the API supplies no phoneme timestamps. It is an
approximation, not forced alignment. Speak sample is available in the preview.
See `models/CAST-CREDITS.md` for the viseme license and character asset credits.

## Room and lamp lighting

Every room and the hallway have a visible wall switch near an entrance, operated
with the usual interact key. Ceiling fixtures, floor lamps and the portable desk
lamp are separately controlled. Lamps emit actual local light; bulbs and shades
dim when switched off. Room-local light selection, a soft shadow key and low
indoor ambient light avoid bright spill from unrelated rooms. Fires and torches
still provide local illumination. The existing flicker/blackout overrides the
fixtures during the murder phase.

The host snapshots `lightsOff`. Shared illumination estimates reduce sight range
and clarity for observations, events and evidence; nearby movement remains
noticeable, hearing is unchanged. The player's visibility grid and interaction
prompts also respond to darkness. Lighting estimates use distance and room/line
of-sight checks, not a GPU pixel readback or a physically exact light meter.


### Device speech compatibility

When Websim returns an empty audio URL or its speech service fails, the player
uses the device's speech engine. Real start/end and word boundary events anchor
the mouth shapes. Voices without word boundary events continue using estimated
letter timing through the whole utterance. That timing is approximate and there is no
measured audio envelope for device speech. If device speech is also unavailable,
the mouth remains neutral and the studio reports that speech is unavailable.
Audio URL playback uses the audio clock and measured envelope as described above.


## Dropped-object gravity and contact

Released hand and pocket items wake immediately, clear carrying/container state,
and fall under 9.8 m/s² gravity. Sleeping props are checked for floor/furniture
support and wake if that support disappears. Snapshot recovery preserves velocity
and sleep state; older snapshots wake loose objects instead of freezing airborne
props. Objects embedded inside furniture resolve through the nearest face,
rather than being lifted onto the furniture's top. Loose-object chair colliders
use seat, back, arm and leg pieces instead of an invisible full-height block.
Visible prop meshes align their lowest solid point with the collision contact
plane when at rest; halo sprites do not affect that alignment. Physics remains
a sphere approximation for loose props, not a full convex rigid-body solver.

### Startup readiness and fiber antialiasing

The initial loading screen now shows progress through all nine human assets, decoded textures, shader compilation and four small first-view GPU draws. Play is enabled after this preparation completes. Robot and human avatar instances are retained in a bounded per-seat pool, so switching from the title's human showcase to a mixed/robot match does not rebuild all rigs and haircut paths. This deliberately performs fixed-asset work before play. TTS, generated paintings, network traffic and other future content remain asynchronous.

Hair fibers use a stable minimum screen footprint with coverage preserved through MSAA alpha-to-coverage, soft ribbon edges, rougher highlights and reduced environment sparkle. No temporal noise/dithering is added. Strand IDs, wind/movement guides and haircut authorization/state are unchanged. The effect needs an antialiased WebGL framebuffer for fractional sample coverage; reflection or devices without MSAA may show a coarser edge.

Readiness and preparation duration are exposed through the game's body data attributes (`assetReadiness`, `preparedHumans`, `preparationMs`) for visual performance review. Smoothness remains dependent on device and scene complexity; these changes do not promise a frame rate on every device.

Props that begin exactly supported by a floor or furniture surface now start asleep, instead of forcing the full untouched scene through gravity/collision settling on the first gameplay ticks. Unsupported objects remain awake and fall normally. Dropping, throwing, moving a support and walking into loose objects still wake them.

The hair shader also derives a smooth tangent along each ribbon and shades it with a view-facing cylindrical-fiber normal. This aligns highlights along the groom instead of highlighting randomly rotated flat ribbon faces. It is a practical cylinder approximation, not the full Marschner scattering/self-shadow model. It adds two small cached geometry attributes and keeps the existing guide/cutting geometry.

Implementation references: [Three.js r160 compilation and texture initialization](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r160/src/renderers/WebGLRenderer.js), [Three.js opaque output alpha](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r160/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/opaque_fragment.glsl.js), and [NVIDIA hair geometry, guide dynamics and fiber rendering discussion](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems2/part-iii-high-quality-rendering/chapter-23-hair-animation-and-rendering-nalu-demo).

The ribbon renderer now faces each fiber toward the camera around its simulated centerline. It uses the physical width rather than the vanishing projected width of an edge-on flat card, with a small minimum coverage for distant fibers. This removes another cause of grainy on/off flashes while preserving the simulated strand center, cut length and body clearance.


### Mansion-first loading, corridor lights and performance diagnostics

Startup displays only the Who Knows? / Murder At Midnight Mansion title, a loading bar and stage text. The house is built and drawn into the background before robot or human assets load. The menu appears after all nine humans, textures, shader preparation and GPU draws complete. Progress represents preparation stages, not byte download percentage.

Prewarming now draws the whole mansion from above and below and samples fixture positions, including mirror captures and the indoor shadow pass. The previous four centre views could leave off-camera geometry buffers untouched until a first visit. Dynamic content and later graphics changes can still require work during play.

Hallway pendants cover every corridor within a 4.5m radius with brighter warm light in normal play. The eight-light shader budget remains fixed. Switches and blackout still turn them off; flickering hallway fixtures retain their previous reduced output rather than multiplying the brighter normal setting. Normal hallway fill is slightly stronger. Visibility texture uploads skip unchanged grids, and lit-room perception avoids redundant prop scans.

`js/clue/performance.js` records startup and one compact 30-second frame summary through relative `POST /api/performance-log` to the Websim database `performance_logs` table. Reports include CPU work, worst update/apply/visibility/draw phases, long tasks, shader/geometry counts and render complexity. These help distinguish first-visit uploads/compilation, simulation stalls and sustained render cost. Frame intervals can include browser scheduling or GPU waits; JavaScript draw timing is not a GPU timer. Hidden tabs are excluded.

Reports use a random per-page session ID without player identity, positions, chat or speech. Each page sends at most 40 reports, with server rate limiting and periodic pruning to seven days / 10,000 records. The project owner can read the newest reports through `GET /api/debug/performance?limit=50`; other users receive 403. `body.dataset.performanceLog` reports persistence status and `performanceSummary` exposes the latest local summary for review. Logging failure never blocks play. Local stub database behaviour does not establish production persistence; review the staged deployment.

The first real demo snapshot now creates and seats its generated task/held items under the loading overlay, before texture/program/buffer warmup. The first full title view is drawn before the menu is revealed. Adaptive resolution uses a sustained three-second signal and a twelve-second resize cooldown to reduce repeated framebuffer reallocation stalls. Owner diagnostics can also be read from Graphics settings → Performance diagnostics.

Indoor rendering conservatively skips furniture in closed rooms other than the viewer's room. Every room with an open door remains eligible; the shell/doors remain intact, and visibility is restored after each split-screen view. This reduces off-screen furniture and shadow draws without hiding a room viewed through an open doorway. Flicker and blackout also reduce indoor ambient fill.

The warmup also advances character/hair dynamics before revealing the menu. Slow-frame reports further split snapshot application into actors, props and world effects, so a later spike can be attributed without inferring it from FPS alone. Report requests time out after ten seconds and never hold the frame loop.

### Fixed hallway illumination

Corridor lantern lighting is now prepared once as a shared world-space irradiance texture. All hallway lamps contribute independently of the camera, rather than entering/leaving the nearest-eight point-light slots. The moving hallway key light is disabled so it cannot create a following bright patch. Room point lights retain their fixed shader budget. Hall switches, flicker and blackout scale the shared light field; no rebake occurs while walking. This is baked diffuse corridor illumination, so hallway lanterns do not cast dynamic character shadows.

Lit hallway floors, walls and ceilings also keep their normal illumination at distance instead of receiving the visibility grid's distance dimming. Actors, evidence and interactable props retain perception limits. Turning the hallway off or entering flicker/blackout restores the dark response.

Performance review uses **Only Human Avatars**, including AI seats and the butler, to include the complete human/hair workload. An all-human review exposed lazy forensic/dirt texture generation at the first task update; the fixed canvas variants, wet texture and decal shader layouts are now prepared under the loading overlay.

The key light's shadow map updates only when that light is contributing. Keeping its intensity at zero alone still runs the depth pass in [Three.js r160](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r160/src/renderers/webgl/WebGLShadowMap.js); manual per-light updates skip this unused work in corridors while keeping the shader light/shadow counts stable. Active room lighting continues to update its shadows.

Mirror captures now require a clear wall/door line of sight from the active viewer and exclude recursive captures. A mirror merely falling inside the camera frustum behind a wall no longer renders another complete mansion. Visible mirrors still capture the current scene; loading prepares their resources in advance. Performance reports include actual mirror capture count.


### Hair, speech playback and coherent conversations

Human guide particles and their velocity history now follow the moving scalp together. Long tips retain reduced inertial lag, with bounded velocity, stronger damping and smoothed guide rotations; short styles use supported groom motion instead of fighting the scalp collider. Hair updates run after carrying/working arm poses. Strand IDs, cut lengths and permissions remain unchanged. Review uses Only Human Avatars.

Character voice playback no longer waits indefinitely on generation or a suspended AudioContext. Browser audio is unlocked on a real click/key gesture. Generated speech plays directly through its audio element, avoiding a silent cross-origin WebAudio routing path. Existing viseme names and mouth geometry remain unchanged; generated speech keeps its time-based lip poses. Device utterances retain boundary/elapsed timing.

Settings → Character voices provides Automatic, Generated and Device speech, male/female installed voice selection, Sound enabled and Test voice. Automatic uses installed device voices when available, with generated fallback; generated speech can fall back to a device voice. Missing device voices, muted sound, playback errors and timeouts are reported honestly. The preview Speak sample uses the same path. Device speech depends on voices installed in that browser/OS.

Starting a conversation cancels both participants' autopilot, stands seated participants and immediately aligns their headings. The renderer can use the local dialogue pair before the next network snapshot arrives. Casual mood/house questions remain available even with an empty notebook. A question about an observed activity keeps that activity as an explicit topic and gets a directly relevant acknowledgement/explanation; it cannot be replaced with a random alibi. Person, room, weapon and whereabouts questions filter facts by their actual subject. ### Pocket permission, opposed D Rolls, and door navigation

Pocket requests now use an authoritative staged exchange: request speech completes, recipient selects a response, response speech completes, then resolution. All participating human seats acknowledge the exact speech-stage token; stale or foreign acknowledgments are ignored. Freely granted permission skips every roll. Refusal or a different conversation choice triggers two opposed d20 results. A requester's score is 45% observation, 35% stealth, 20% persuasion; a responder's is 50% observation, 30% skepticism, 20% boldness. Bonus is round((score − 0.5) × 8). Scores >= 0.75 roll with advantage, <= 0.35 with disadvantage, otherwise normally. The requester needs a strictly higher adjusted total; ties preserve refusal. Natural 1/20 do not override that comparison.

A successful refused request produces a private, uncertain pocket glimpse without increasing responder suspicion or damaging trust. A successful defender gains suspicion of the requester; a failed requester receives no contents or imagined observation. Freely shown contents are direct observations. Only the two pockets are inspected; hands are excluded. Covert glimpses stay private and uncertain in notes and are excluded from ordinary share choices. Item lists are delivered only to the requester's seat. Both sides see their own roll, the opposing total, and their actual advantage/disadvantage samples. Each character's soul now provides unique yes, no, unaware, guarded and glimpse fallback wording; generated private thoughts are constrained to the actual outcome. Resolved exchanges end the conversation and count against the existing once-per-pair-per-night check. Interrupted or timed-out speech stages do not roll or reveal items. When audio is unavailable, a bounded reading delay precedes acknowledgment. Audible nearby witnesses of NPC-to-NPC requests can acknowledge ambient speech; completely unobserved exchanges use an estimated speaking duration.

NPC routes account for moved pushable furniture and fully open unbarricaded door leaves, preserving blocked doors. Start/goal snapping stays in the actual room so a coarse navigation node cannot skip a required door crossing. A measured lack of progress triggers a tip detour or a bounded local collision-respecting A*; a guest boxed behind a leaf can close it temporarily, escape, then reopen when crossing. Tidiness still influences closing, but guests wait for bodies and planned crossings to clear. Offline movement checks cover 168 open-leaf offset starts (145 passed before, 168 after) and 42 closed-door crossings (42 passed). These checks do not guarantee every possible multi-guest crowd/furniture arrangement. Help now documents pocket contests, speech controls, conversation choices and navigation behavior.
